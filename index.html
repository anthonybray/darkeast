The Thesis

Everyone knows the East End.
Nobody tells this side of it.

DARK EAST is a guided-tour concept built on the true, darker memory of Whitechapel, Aldgate and Spitalfields — the corner of London where a city's forgotten dead, its poorest and its most infamous all left their mark on the same few cramped streets.

"There were dead carts going through, and people crying, and the doors and windows shut…"

In the summer of 1665, plague swept eastward from St Giles through the teeming lanes of Whitechapel and Stepney on its way to the City. As many as a hundred thousand Londoners died — perhaps a quarter of the whole city — and the East End buried them in open pits.

Two centuries later, in the autumn of 1888, the same quarter became the stage for the Whitechapel murders — five women killed on the poorest streets in the capital, their killer never found. The case forced Victorian Britain to look at the poverty beneath its imperial wealth, and it has never lost its hold.

DARK EAST does not sensationalise. Both walks are historically grounded, led on the actual streets, and told with care for the people — the living and the dead — whose stories made this place.

The Two Walks

Two evenings. Two centuries.
One neighbourhood's long shadow.

Each walk is a self-contained 2-hour guided evening tour of around two miles, starting at a landmark and ending on the historic streets where the story belongs.

Tour I
1665
The Plague Walk

The Summer of the Great Mortality

Blood and quicklime in the alleys of Whitechapel

Walk the ground where the Great Plague took hold. From the plague pits beneath what is now a Whitechapel supermarket to the famous bills of mortality, hear how an East End parish lost five times its normal dead in a single season — and what the dead-cart men, the shut doors and the silence truly sounded like.

On the walk

  • Whitechapel & Aldgate burial pits
  • The 1665 bills of mortality
  • Plague Orders & the walled City
  • Stepney parish & the outparishes
  • Contemporary plague engravings
  • The quicklime & the dead carts

Tour II
1888
The Ripper Walk

Five Women of Whitechapel

The unsolved mystery — and the poverty it exposed

Follow the trail of the 1888 Whitechapel murders through the streets of Spitalfields and Whitechapel: where the victims were last seen, where their bodies were found, and the manhunt that never closed the case. A walk about five real women, told with restraint — and the appalling conditions that made the murders possible.

Why dark, why now

The whole dark map of the East End.

Beyond the two headline walks, a rich field of stories runs under these streets. The concept is built to grow — from haunted Thames docks to Tower Hill's scaffold, to the occult and the underworld of the old East End.
